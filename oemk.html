Olla ei mistään kotoisinOf No Origin

I came across the Finnish phrase olla ei mistään kotoisin when discussing with researcher and artist Dr. Lena Seraphin. Literally meaning “from nowhere,” it can imply low quality or no worth. What does it mean to come from nowhere? Who decides whose lives and histories are worth archiving?

For most of my life, my great-grandmother’s past was a mystery to me. She died when I was ten and rarely spoke about her life. Even her wedding photograph was lost during the upheavals of twentieth-century China.

I began trying to find her through records left by other people. Yet while the men in her family entered historical records, my great-grandmother’s life almost disappeared. She was widowed early and raised her daughter alone by washing clothes for others at the riverbank.

When no direct image survives, I work with found archival photographs as proxies for what is missing. My work moves between personal and collective histories, photographic fragments, oral histories, sculptural forms and publication. It does not attempt to reconstruct a complete history; some pieces connect, and some do not.
